Kawasaki didn't build the H2R to win races — they built it to redefine what a motorcycle could physically be. Track-only. Supercharged. A 998cc inline-four producing 310 horsepower in ram-air conditions, wrapped in carbon fibre winglets and fed by the first centrifugal supercharger ever fitted to a series-production bike.

Supercharger
FORCED
INDUCTION
Kawasaki's engineers designed the supercharger entirely in-house — a centrifugal compressor with nine impeller blades machined to aerospace tolerances. It spins at up to 130,000 RPM, force-feeding the engine a dense wall of compressed air. The result: a torque curve with no dip, no lag, no mercy.

Aerodynamics
LIFT IS
THE ENEMY
At 300 km/h, air becomes a physical force that can pitch a rider into oblivion. Kawasaki's wind-tunnel engineers shaped the H2R's carbon fibre body to fight that force at every corner. Dual winglets and a lower splitter generate enough downforce to keep both tyres planted.

The Legend
H2R
FOREVER
In September 2016, Guy Martin rode a Kawasaki Ninja H2R to 331.15 km/h — a British land speed record. Not a prototype. A production machine. No other road-derived motorcycle has ever gone faster. This is where engineering ends and mythology begins.
